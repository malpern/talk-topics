LEVEL 01

Your first game

  1. Open your current build.On Mac, open Waka from Applications. On Windows, open Waka.exe. On Linux, open Waka from a graphical desktop with ALSA and Vulkan drivers.
  2. Press 1 for solo.Wait for the opening jingle. The moving demo is not your game; starting solo puts you in control.
  3. Use the arrows or WASD.Collect every pellet, avoid the ghosts, and take an energizer to turn the chase around. Request a corner turn before you reach the opening.
Already know the game?Press 2 for two people on one machine, or G to host co-op with the bundled image-only agent.

Network play cannot be paused or restarted by one player. Use Escape to leave, then start a new session.

LEVEL 03

A mouse can be your joystick

  1. Press and hold anywhere inside the game window.The press point becomes the center of your controller.
  2. Drag up, down, left, or right.The strongest direction chooses your turn. Small hand movements and near-diagonal jitter are filtered.
  3. Release, or keep steering around that center.Releasing keeps the last direction queued. A new click gives you a fresh center.

Moving the pointer without holding the button does nothing. Leaving the window, switching to the keyboard, pausing, or changing focus ends the gesture.

This is directional steering: clicking a destination does not navigate there automatically. Mouse and keyboard use the same legal turns and the same online input deadlines.

LEVEL 04

Play with someone else

On one machine

Press 2. Yellow uses WASD or the mouse; cyan uses the arrows. You share pellets, power-ups and five lives, with separate scores.

On two machines

Use matching builds. Both machines need a reachable network path; for remote play, use your existing Tailscale connection.

  1. The host presses H.Choose 1 Equalized or 2 Responsive. Keep the host window running.
  2. The other player presses J.Enter the host’s reachable name or address. Port 24880 is the default; append :port if your host uses another.
  3. Wait for the shared countdown.The title tells you which player you control and which timing mode is active.

When one player dies, the other can continue. Respawning uses the shared life pool. A disconnect or checksum mismatch stops the session visibly rather than letting the two games drift apart.

LEVEL 05

Bring an agent along

Press G from local play or the title screen. Waka starts the host and bundled reference policy for you. You control yellow; the agent controls cyan.

The reference agent locates players, ghosts and pellets in images, then plans paths. It is a baseline partner, not a perfect player. You still share lives, and it can make mistakes.

Agent games use Equalized timing.Expect deliberate network-style delay even when the agent runs on your Mac. Solo play and ordinary local co-op do not use that buffer.

Building your own partner? The agent guide covers the external process interface and custom policies.

LEVEL 06

What the timing modes mean

ModeBehaviorDefault buffer cost
Solo / local co-opInput reaches the next simulation update.No network buffer.
Responsive networkUniform input delay; display confirmed frames on arrival.12 ticks / 198 ms input delay.
Equalized networkUniform input delay plus a common presentation schedule.12 + 12 ticks / about 396 ms.

These are configured buffer durations, not measured end-to-end latency guarantees. Display, scheduling and transport add overhead. A turn also waits for a legal opening in the maze.

Agent sessions require Equalized. The default for human-only hosting remains an explicit choice in the lobby. Physical display/reaction-window qualification across machines is still in progress.

LEVEL 07

If something feels wrong

  • Keys seem ignored: make sure you started a game with 1 or Enter rather than watching the demo, and bring the game window into focus.
  • A turn feels late: try reversing in a straight corridor. If reversal is quick but a corner waits, the direction may be queued for the next legal opening. Check whether you are in a network timing mode.
  • Cannot join: check the host address, matching build, Tailscale reachability, and local-network/firewall permission. Keep the host open.
  • Fairness deadline missed: the session stopped because it could not maintain its timing bound. Close heavy background work and reconnect; do not assume the game continued fairly.
  • Agent unavailable: install the complete current package, not only a copied executable. The app needs its bundled policy helper.
  • Linux startup problem: use an unlocked graphical session with Vulkan support. A locked or headless desktop may not present frames.
